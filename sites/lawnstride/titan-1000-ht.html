Built for the job

Why a tracked dumper beats a barrow and a plank

700 mm across

The number that decides whether the job is possible. It goes through a standard doorway, down a terrace alley and through a side gate, fully loaded.

High tip, not low tip

1300 mm of discharge height puts the load over the wall of a builder's skip. A low-tip machine means shovelling the same spoil twice.

Tracks, not wheels

Low ground pressure spreads a tonne over the whole track. It crosses a finished lawn or wet clay without cutting ruts you have to make good.

Loaded on a slope

Hydrostatic drive with independent track control holds a 25° gradient under full load — the situation where a wheeled barrow gets away from you.

Dead-man bar

Release the bar and drive stops. On a sloping garden with a tonne behind you, that is the feature that matters most.

385 kg unladen

Under the limit for a braked car trailer. No plant lorry, no HGV, no waiting on a transport slot to get to site.

Questions

About the Titan 1000 HT

Will it really fit through a doorway?

700 mm across the tracks. A standard UK internal door is 762 mm and an external door 838 mm, so yes — but measure the narrowest point on the route, which is usually a gate post or a meter box, not the door.

Is 1000 kg realistic, or is that a dry figure?

1000 kg is the rated payload on level ground. On a gradient you should work to about 700 kg for stability. The skip holds 0.38 m³ heaped, which is roughly 700 kg of wet soil or 950 kg of hardcore — volume runs out before weight does on most muck-away work.

Petrol or diesel?

Petrol, deliberately. At this size a diesel adds around 40 kg and pushes the width past the 700 mm doorway limit that is the whole point of the machine. The 13 hp unit is electric start and turns over cold.

Can it climb a steep garden?

25 degrees laden, which covers most sloping gardens. Above that, reduce the load rather than trusting the figure — and always drive up and down the slope, never across it.

How do I get it on site?

385 kg unladen goes on a braked car trailer with the folding ramps supplied in the crate. Check your trailer's plated capacity and your licence entitlement before you load it.
